In Brief

The system is sized starting from total daily energy consumption in watt-hours (Wh): required solar panel capacity is computed by dividing daily energy by peak sun hours (PSH) and system efficiency; battery storage capacity is sized by multiplying daily consumption by days of autonomy and dividing by battery voltage and depth of discharge (DoD); and continuous inverter rating is sized with a 25% safety margin above simultaneous peak load.

Engineering Notice: Results are provided for educational and preliminary estimation purposes. Electrical installations, high-voltage PV strings, and battery energy storage systems must be engineered, verified, and installed by a certified solar/electrical engineer in accordance with local national electrical codes.

Engineering Calculation Methodology

Off-grid and hybrid solar PV systems are mathematically balanced to achieve energy equilibrium across seasonal solar irradiance and daily consumption:

1. Daily Energy Consumption (E)

E = Σ (P × N × h)

Calculated in Watt-hours (Wh). The sum of each device's rated power P multiplied by quantity N and daily operational hours h.

2. Required Solar Array (Wp)

Wp = E / [ 0.90 × (PSH × η) ]

Where 0.90 is typical inverter efficiency, PSH is peak sun hours (worst-month recommended), and η is system performance ratio (~80%).

3. Battery Capacity (Ah)

Ah = (E / 0.90 × Days) / (V × DoD)

Where V is battery bank voltage, Days is autonomy without sun, and DoD is depth of discharge (80% for LiFePO4, 50% for Lead-Acid).

4. Inverter Power Rating

Pinv = Ppeak × 1.25

Continuous inverter wattage must exceed total simultaneous peak load by at least 25%, with high surge capability for motor inrush.

5. MPPT Charge Controller

Icc ≥ (Parray / Vbatt) × 1.25

Current rating of the solar charge controller must safely convey full array power with an extra 25% safety headroom.

6. Recommended System Voltage

12V (<1.5 kWh) · 24V (1.5–4 kWh) · 48V (>4 kWh)

Higher DC voltage substantially reduces circuit current, conductor thickness, line voltage drop, and electrical power losses.
