In a Nutshell

The average output voltage of a PWM signal equals the HIGH logic level voltage multiplied by the duty cycle (Vavg = Vhigh × D). The maximum timer bit resolution is the floor of the binary logarithm of the clock-to-PWM frequency ratio (bits = ⌊log2(fclk / fpwm)⌋). For instance, an ESP32 (80 MHz timer clock) at 5 kHz yields up to 13-bit resolution (4096 of 8191).

Method of Calculation & Mathematical Formulas

Pulse Width Modulation (PWM) is a modulation technique that encodes an analog voltage level into the duty cycle of a fixed-frequency digital square wave.

1. Average Voltage (Vavg)

The DC equivalent average voltage delivered to an electrical load (such as a DC motor or LED) is the product of the HIGH logic level voltage and the duty cycle fraction:

Vavg = Vhigh × D = Vhigh × (Duty% ÷ 100)

2. Period (T) and Pulse Widths (thigh, tlow)

The total signal period is the inverse of the PWM frequency, which is split into the active HIGH and idle LOW durations:

T = 1 ÷ fpwm
thigh = T × D
tlow = T − thigh = T × (1 − D)

3. Maximum Bit Resolution vs. Switching Frequency

Timer resolution represents how many discrete discrete duty cycle steps the hardware timer counter can count within one PWM period. As the PWM frequency increases, fewer timer clock ticks occur per cycle, decreasing the maximum resolution:

bits = ⌊log2(fclk ÷ fpwm)⌋

For example, on an ESP32 with fclk = 80 MHz at fpwm = 5 kHz: 80,000,000 / 5,000 = 16,000 ticks. Then ⌊log2(16000)⌋ = 13 bits (providing 8,192 discrete duty levels).

4. Timer Duty Register Count

The integer value to write into the hardware compare match register (e.g. ledcWrite or OCRnA) for an n-bit resolution:

Register Value = round(D × (2n − 1))

Practical Worked Engineering Examples

Example 1: Standard ESP32 5 kHz LEDC Dimming

An ESP32 running at 3.3V controls an LED strip with 50% duty cycle at 5 kHz using its 80 MHz clock:

• Vavg = 3.3V × 0.50 = 1.65 V
• Period T = 1 / 5000 = 200 µs • thigh = 100 µs
• Maximum Resolution = ⌊log2(80M / 5k)⌋ = 13 bits • Duty count = 4096 of 8191

Example 2: Inaudible Silent DC Motor Drive (20 kHz)

To eliminate annoying acoustic coil whine from a robotic motor driver, the PWM frequency is set to 20 kHz (ultrasonic) on an ESP32:

• Ticks per cycle = 80,000,000 ÷ 20,000 = 4,000
• Max Resolution = ⌊log2(4000)⌋ = 11 bits (counts 0 to 2047)
• Period T = 50 µs • At 50% duty: thigh = 25 µs • Reg count = 1024

Example 3: Arduino Uno 8-Bit analogWrite() on Pin 9

An Arduino Uno (5V, 16 MHz) driving an analogWrite(9, 128) signal:

• fpwm = 490 Hz • Period T = 2.04 ms
• Duty Cycle = 128 / 255 = 50.2% • Vavg = 2.51 V
• thigh = 1.02 ms • Fixed 8-bit hardware counter (0 to 255)

Frequently Asked Questions

Why is 20 kHz or 25 kHz preferred for driving DC motors and fans?
Human hearing typically spans 20 Hz to 20,000 Hz. When electric motors are driven by PWM frequencies in the audible spectrum (e.g. 500 Hz or 2 kHz), the rapid magnetic flux switching causes the motor coils to physically vibrate like an acoustic speaker, emitting an annoying high-pitched hum. Driving at ≥ 20 kHz pushes the switching acoustic noise completely above human perception.
What is the difference between Average Voltage and RMS Voltage in PWM?
Average voltage (Vavg = Vhigh × D) governs inductive and linear mechanical systems like DC motor speed or low-pass filtered analog voltages. RMS voltage (Vrms = Vhigh × √D) governs thermal and power dissipation in resistive loads like heating elements and incandescent lamps, where instantaneous power is proportional to voltage squared (P = V2 / R).
How do I convert a PWM signal into a clean analog DC voltage (PWM DAC)?
Connect an RC low-pass filter (a series resistor R followed by a shunt capacitor C to ground). Choose the filter's cutoff frequency fc = 1 / (2πRC) to be at least 10 to 20 times lower than the PWM frequency. The filter blocks the high-frequency switching carrier while letting the average DC voltage pass through with minimal ripple.
Why does increasing PWM frequency decrease timer bit resolution?
A digital timer increments its internal counter once per clock tick (1 / fclk). The number of total ticks that can occur during one PWM period (1 / fpwm) is fclk / fpwm. If you double the PWM frequency, the period is cut in half, so the counter has half as many ticks to count between zero and top, reducing the available bit depth by 1 bit.
