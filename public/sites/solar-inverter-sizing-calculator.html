Solar Inverter Sizing Calculator

Size continuous wattage (W), motor locked-rotor surge (W), apparent power (kVA), and full-load DC battery current with safety margins.

In Brief

Continuous inverter power rating is chosen approximately 25% higher than the total simultaneous running load, while the inverter's peak surge rating must withstand the starting inrush current of the largest motor or compressor, which typically reaches 3× or more of its rated power.

Engineering Notice: Results are provided for educational and preliminary estimation purposes. Electrical installations, high-voltage PV strings, and power electronics must be designed, protected, and installed by a certified electrical engineer in accordance with local safety standards.

Engineering Calculation Methodology

Solar inverters convert direct current (DC) from batteries or solar panels into alternating current (AC). Sizing requires accommodating both steady-state thermal loads and short-duration inductive motor inrush currents:

1. Continuous Power with Safety Margin (m)

Pcont = P × (1 + m)

Continuous wattage rating with safety headroom (typically m = 25%) prevents the inverter from operating at thermal maximum capacity.

2. Peak Motor Starting Surge

Psurge = (P − Pm) + (k × Pm)

Where Pm is the wattage of the largest motor and k is its starting locked-rotor inrush multiplier (typically 3× to 7×).

3. Apparent Power Rating (S)

S = Pcont / cosφ

Many commercial inverters are rated in Volt-Amps (VA) or kVA. For inductive loads with power factor cosφ = 0.8, VA exceeds Watts by 25%.

4. Full-Load DC Battery Current (Idc)

Idc = P / (0.90 × Vbatt)

Current drawn from the DC battery bank assuming typical 90% inverter power conversion efficiency.

Pure Sine Wave vs. Modified Sine Wave:

Always select a Pure Sine Wave Inverter for domestic, off-grid, and commercial installations. Modified sine wave (square wave) inverters cause high harmonic distortion, excessive overheating, buzzing noises, and premature motor failure in refrigerators, pumps, fans, compressors, and sensitive electronic controllers.

Practical Benchmark Problem

Benchmark: 1,500 W Total Running · 400 W Fridge Compressor (3× Surge) · 24 V DC

A home system running 1,500 W of simultaneous appliances, with a 400 W refrigerator compressor having a 3× starting multiplier, powered from a 24 V battery bank at 0.8 power factor:

1. Continuous Demand 1500 × 1.25 = 1,875 W
2. Motor Surge Peak (1500 − 400) + (3 × 400) = 2,300 W
3. Apparent Power 1875 / 0.8 = 2.34 kVA (2,344 VA)
4. Inverter Model 2 kW (2.5 kVA) Pure Sine Wave
5. Battery Full Load 1500 / (0.90 × 24) = 69 A
