Op-Amp Circuit Design Guidelines

Rail Saturation & Clipping

A standard op-amp (e.g. LM741, TL072, LM358) cannot swing all the way to its power rails. Internal transistor drops limit the peak swing to approximately 1.5V below V+ and V−. Rail-to-rail (RRO) op-amps can swing within 50–200 mV of the rails.

Input Impedance Differences

In the inverting amplifier, input impedance equals $R_{\text{in}}$ (typically 10 kΩ) because the inverting node is a virtual ground. In the non-inverting amplifier, the signal feeds directly into the non-inverting terminal, yielding nearly infinite input impedance ($> 10^{11}\,\Omega$).

Gain-Bandwidth Product (GBW)

Op-amps have a finite Gain-Bandwidth Product (e.g. 1 MHz for LM741, 3 MHz for TL072). The maximum operating frequency drops inversely with closed-loop gain: f_max ≈ GBW / |Av|. At a gain of 10×, a 1 MHz op-amp operates linearly up to only ~100 kHz.

Resistor Value Sizing Rules

Keep feedback resistors between 1 kΩ and 100 kΩ. Values below 1 kΩ draw excessive output current from the op-amp, while values above 1 MΩ increase thermal noise and susceptibility to stray capacitance.
