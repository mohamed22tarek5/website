In a Nutshell

A Duty Cycle (or Power Cycle) is the fraction of one complete period during which an active signal or electronic power switch is turned ON (D = Ton / T). In switching DC-DC power converters, the duty cycle creates the fundamental voltage conversion relationship between the supply voltage (Vsupply) and the output voltage (Vout).

Fundamental Formulas & Operating Principles

The Duty Cycle (denoted as D) is the fundamental control variable in pulse-width modulation and switched-mode power supplies (SMPS). It dictates the steady-state voltage transformation based on the Principle of Inductor Volt-Second Balance.

1. Periodic Pulse Waveform

For any periodic pulse train of frequency f and period T = 1 / f = Ton + Toff:

Duty Cycle:  D = Ton ÷ T = Ton ÷ (Ton + Toff) = Ton × f
Percentage: D(%) = D × 100%

2. Step-Up (Boost) Converter

A Boost converter steps up a lower input supply voltage to a higher output voltage (Vout > Vsupply). Applying volt-second balance across the inductor in Continuous Conduction Mode (CCM):

Voltage Transfer:  Vout ÷ Vsupply = 1 ÷ (1 − D)
Ideal Duty Cycle:  D = (Vout − Vsupply) ÷ Vout = 1 − (Vsupply ÷ Vout)
With Efficiency η:  D = 1 − ((Vsupply × η) ÷ Vout)

3. Step-Down (Buck) Converter

A Buck converter produces an output voltage strictly lower than the input supply (Vout < Vsupply):

Voltage Transfer:  Vout ÷ Vsupply = D
Ideal Duty Cycle:  D = Vout ÷ Vsupply
With Efficiency η:  D = Vout ÷ (Vsupply × η)

4. Buck-Boost & Ćuk Converters

These topologies can step up or step down the magnitude of the supply voltage while inverting the output polarity:

Voltage Transfer:  |Vout| ÷ Vsupply = D ÷ (1 − D)
Ideal Duty Cycle:  D = |Vout| ÷ (|Vout| + Vsupply)

Practical Worked Engineering Examples

Example 1: 5V USB to 12V DC Step-Up (Boost Converter)

A USB 5V rail powers a 12V cooling fan using a boost switching regulator at 100 kHz:

• D = (12 − 5) / 12 = 7 / 12 = 58.33%
• Period T = 10 µs • Ton = 5.83 µs, Toff = 4.17 µs
• With 90% efficiency: D = 1 − (5 × 0.90 / 12) = 62.5%

Example 2: 12V Car Battery to 5V USB Step-Down (Buck Converter)

Stepping down 12V vehicle power to 5.0V phone charging:

• D = 5.0 / 12.0 = 41.67% • Ton = 4.17 µs of a 10 µs cycle (at 100 kHz)

Example 3: 100 kHz Periodic Pulse Signal

A signal is active for 2.5 µs and inactive for 7.5 µs:

• Total Period T = 2.5 + 7.5 = 10 µs (100 kHz)
• D = 2.5 / 10 = 25.0% • Average Voltage = 0.25 × Vpeak

Frequently Asked Questions

Why is a Boost Converter's duty cycle practically limited to ~85%?
While the theoretical formula Vout / Vin = 1 / (1 − D) implies infinite voltage gain as D approaches 1.0 (100%), real-world circuits contain parasitic resistances: inductor DC resistance (DCR), switch Rds(on), and diode voltage drop. As D exceeds 85%, the switch spends almost the entire cycle shorting the inductor to ground, leaving negligible time to transfer energy to the output capacitor. The resulting I2R conduction losses cause the output voltage and efficiency to collapse rapidly.
Can a Boost converter step down voltage if Vsupply > Vout?
No. In a standard Boost topology, there is an uninterrupted forward path from Vsupply through the inductor and the freewheeling diode directly to Vout. If Vsupply > Vout, current will conduct uncontrolled through the diode to the output. To step up and step down flexibly, you must use a Buck-Boost, Ćuk, or 4-Switch Buck-Boost converter.
How does duty cycle relate to power in heating and lighting?
Average electrical power in a resistive load (such as a heater or incandescent filament) scales with the square of RMS voltage: Pavg = Vrms2 / R = (Vpeak2 × D) / R. Therefore, the average power delivered is directly proportional to the duty cycle D. A 50% duty cycle delivers exactly 50% of the maximum continuous power.
